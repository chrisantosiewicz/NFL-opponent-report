# Seattle, Week 8 (Bears at Seahawks, Mon Nov 2)
Draft: written from the data by Claude for Chris to edit. Numbers are through Week 7; roster moves through Nov 1.
## Stopping their offense
Seattle wants to run, and it hasn't worked. On early downs they run more than the situation calls for (26th in pass rate over expected) and are under center 55% of the time, but their early-down runs are 31st at −0.26 EPA per run on 145 plays. Their answer was to trade for G Quenton Nelson on Oct 27, a day after G Christian Haynes (60% of their snaps) left for Miami. Tonight is Nelson's first game, so none of their run numbers include him. Our answer is DT Jeffery Simmons, also debuting after the Oct 29 trade. The plan starts there: win early downs with the front, Simmons and the interior, and don't pull a safety down to do it.
The safety stays back because the passing game runs through one man. Jaxon Smith-Njigba gets 36% of their targets (78 of 215), the highest share of any player in the NFL, and it hasn't faded (35% over the last 4 games). He averages +0.75 EPA per target and everyone else they throw to averages −0.11. With our secondary where it is, the coverage plan is JSN first. If we take away the run without giving him one-on-ones, they're left throwing to players who haven't hurt anyone.
That forces 3rd & long, which is already where they live: 52 of their 90 third downs have been 7+ to go, and they're league average there (+0.01 EPA per play). Third down is also where they lean on JSN hardest, with 28 of 62 third-down targets overall and 11 of 20 on 3rd & 1-6. Late in halves they've been the 2nd-worst offense in the league (−0.36 EPA per play), so keep everything in front and make someone other than JSN beat us.
## Attacking their defense
This is a defense that doesn't give much away between the 20s, so the plan is patience. On 1st and 2nd down they allow −0.25 EPA per run, the fewest in the league (league −0.09), and only 28% of runs against them succeed. Passing early isn't much easier: −0.05 per dropback, 4th-fewest (league +0.10). They've been even stingier over the last 4 games. Short-yardage is a wall too, with 3rd & 1-3 plays averaging −1.05 per run and −0.93 per dropback on a small sample, so the real goal on early downs is staying out of 3rd & long, where they allow −0.34 per dropback (6th-fewest).
Where they have given ground is in tight and to the backs. Inside their 20 they've allowed +0.55 per dropback (3rd-most) and +0.19 per run (4th-most), though on only 27 plays. In coverage, running backs are the soft spot: +0.19 EPA per target, 5th-most in the league, against a league average of +0.02. Wide receivers have done the least against them of any position: −0.03 per target (3rd-fewest) and 92 receiving yards per game, the fewest in the NFL. That matters because Luther Burden III gets 21% of our targets, so his touches should come on our terms (motion, quick game, screens) rather than forcing him into their best coverage.
## What to look for on defense
- Early runs behind Nelson. If Seattle runs inside on its first two series, that's the trade showing up. See whether Simmons is winning those snaps before we commit an extra defender.
- Under center on 1st and 2nd down. That's 55% of their early downs. They've run on 65% of those snaps (league 66%), so it's a lean, not a giveaway, and those plays average −0.14 EPA.
- Where JSN lines up on 3rd down and in the red zone (12 of their 29 red-zone targets). Bracket or shade him there; he also has 12 of their 22 deep targets.
- Whether they stay patient. If the run stalls and they fall behind, expect more JSN. They were average when trailing by 8+ (+0.05 EPA per play) but have been worse over the last 4 games.
## What to look for on offense
- Whether early runs get anything. If 1st-down runs are stuffed on the first two series, lean on quick passes and the backs in the pass game rather than banging into the league's best early-down run defense.
- Backs as receivers. Checkdowns, screens and routes out of the backfield are where Seattle has been beaten most (+0.19 EPA per target, 5th-most). See how they cover the first one.
- How Burden is used. Seattle has held wide receivers to the fewest yards in the league, so look for him on schemed touches rather than isolated one-on-ones.
- Red-zone trips. They've allowed +0.55 per dropback inside the 20 (3rd-most), so when we get there, it's a chance to be aggressive throwing.
- Third-down distance. They're very hard to beat on 3rd & 7+ (−0.34 per dropback), so track how often we're in it.
